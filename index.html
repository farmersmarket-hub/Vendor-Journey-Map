→ Starting Point: Recapping Your Journey to This Point
  • You received an invitation to apply and completed the application process

Module A: Verifying & Updating Your Vendor Registration Information

Module 1A
A Types of Vendors
  • FarmerGrows/harvests agricultural products and sells produce directly – sells produce directly at market
  • Value-Added Producer (VAP)Transforms raw ingredients into value-added products (jams, sauces, baked goods, etc.) – transforms raw ingredients into products
  • RetailerResells packaged goods at the market – resells packaged goods
  • Hot-Food VendorPrepares and sells ready-to-eat foods at the market – prepares ready-to-eat foods
  • EducatorProvides educational services, workshops, and trainings at the market – provides educational services and workshops
  • AggregatorCollects and distributes produce from multiple sources/farms – collects and distributes from multiple sources
A Single vs. Hybrid User Types
  • Single user type: You are only a Vendor in the system
  • Hybrid user types: You may also serve in another role simultaneously:
  • Manager-VendorA manager who also sells as a vendor at the market they manage — you manage AND sell at market
  • Customer-VendorA customer/neighbor who also sells as a vendor — you shop AND sell at market
  • Trainee-VendorA trainee who is also approved to sell as a vendor — you are in training AND sell at market
i
If you have a hybrid role, you can switch between role views using the role tabs that appear at the top of your tutorial.
A Vendor ID, Vendor PIN & Modification
  • Vendor ID: Your unique system identifier — used on all forms and to look up your records. Format: VEND-12345
  • Vendor PIN: Your personal number for logging into the Transaction Form on market day. Keep this secure and memorable.
  • To update your registration info, use the Vendor Request/Modification Form ↗

Module 1B
B What Are Required Documents?
  • Types: W-9, business licenses, food handler permits, insurance certificates, and other certifications depending on your vendor type
  • Examples: State food license, organic certification, farmers market permit, liability insurance
  • Requirements vary by vendor type and program — check with your program manager

For more information, consult your program manager. You can find their contact information in the Program section of your app, on the website, or in Module C: Understanding Your Program below.

Other Programs for Vendors

CSA, Supply Chain, Trainings Modules 4, 5, 6
4 Module 4: Participating in a Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) Program

What is a CSA Program? Community Supported Agriculture connects vendors directly with enrollees who receive regular produce shares. CSA programs are managed through specific contracts and have their own IDs and forms.

  • CSA Program IDUnique identifier for a specific CSA program under a contract — identifies which CSA program you are participating in
  • CSA Enrollee IDUnique identifier for a customer enrolled in the CSA program — identifies each enrolled customer/member

Relevant Forms:

  • CSA Donor Form ↗
  • CSA Set-Up Form ↗
  • CSA Enrollment Form ↗
  • CSA Pick Up Form ↗

Vendor Roles in CSA:

  • Selling Excess Produce Onsite: Sell excess produce before or after the market as a wholesale transaction (if you are a Farmer or VAP)
  • Participating via Supply Chain: Contribute produce to the CSA through the supply chain aggregation process
  • CSA Admin/Logistics Support: Assist the manager with CSA administration and logistics operations (e.g., packing, distribution coordination)
5 Module 5: Participating in a Supply Chain Program

Key IDs and Tools:

  • Route IDUnique identifier for a delivery route in the supply chain — identifies a specific delivery route
  • Produce IDIdentifier linking specific produce items to their source vendor — links produce to its source vendor
  • Aggregation IDIdentifier for a combined load from multiple vendors at an aggregation point — identifies a combined load from multiple vendors
  • QR Codes — used for scanning and tracking throughout the supply chain

Supply Chain Operations:

  • Route creation and amendment: Creating new delivery routes and modifying existing ones
  • Supply chain load: Loading produce onto a delivery route
  • Supply chain aggregation: Combining produce from multiple vendors at a collection point
  • Aggregator vendor: A vendor who serves as an aggregation point — collecting and distributing produce from multiple sources/farms
i
Farmers, VAPs, Aggregators, and Retailers can all participate in supply chain programs. School and institutional sales are common use cases.
6 Module 6: Participating in a Training Program — As an Educator

If your vendor type is Educator, you may conduct training sessions as part of a training program. This module covers your role as an educator vendor within the Homegrown system.

  • Conducting trainings: Lead workshops, classes, or certification sessions for trainees
  • Food safety workshops: Teach food handling, storage, and safety best practices
  • Certification programs: Facilitate programs that lead to formal certifications
  • Business development: Help other vendors and trainees with business skills, marketing, and operations
  • Scheduling & enrollment: Coordinate with the program manager on class schedules and trainee enrollment
  • Tracking participation: Log training sessions and attendance through the system
Educator vendors may also sell at the market in addition to conducting trainings. If you have a hybrid role (e.g., Farmer-Educator), you will see both vendor and educator responsibilities in your journey.
These programs have separate enrollment — ask your program manager for details. Each program has its own ID, forms, and enrollment process.

Module 3: During and After the Market

3 parts Operations Module 3: A, B, C
A Before the Market Begins
  • Verify you know the time and location of the market
  • Verify your Vendor PIN is ready and your status is Approved-Active
  • Vendor check-in: Check in at the manager check-in table OR use vendor self-check-in if enabled through your contract. Your manager will confirm which method is used at your market.
  • Set up your table and display your products
  • Open the Transaction Form ↗ and run a test transaction
  • Locate the vendor wallet to see a live view of your balance
  • Locate the market manager staff onsite who can help answer questions
B During the Market

Customer Types:

  • New customers: First-time visitors registering at the market
  • Returning customers: Have a Customer IDUnique customer identifier (e.g., REACH-12345) with a virtual ID card and virtual ID card
  • Guest customers: For periodic markets where customers are not registered — use Guest ID each time; no benefits can be used
  • Scan QR code or manual entry of Customer ID in transaction form

Using the Transaction Form: (Arcade Video Walkthrough ↗)

  • Retail vs. Wholesale transactions — select the correct type
  • Enter product information from your pre-loaded inventory
  • Electronic payments (via Zelle, etc.)
  • Paying with benefits: Set through contract form; allocation policy and eligibility determine who has benefits
Customer benefits shown/displayed are all the benefits they have — there are no errors. Customers cannot spend more than they have unless they pay out of pocket.
Vendor-Type Benefit Restrictions: Depending on your vendor type, certain benefits may not be accepted at your stall. For example, Retailers typically cannot accept SNAP, Veggie Bucks, or other subsidy-based benefits — only Farmers, VAPs, and Hot-Food Vendors can. These restrictions are set through the contract form and enforced through the transaction form validations. Check with your program manager for the specific benefit restrictions that apply to your vendor type and market.
B+C Completing Sales & End of Market

After each transaction:

  • Confirmation screen upon submission — don't miss this!
  • Wait screen → Submission screen → Verify money in mobile wallet

Wholesale selling:

  • To market manager or sponsor (for CSA and/or supply chain)
  • To sponsor as institutional purchaser
  • To another vendor (only retail transactions for vendor-to-vendor sales)
Restriction: Vendors selling to themselves and family (especially via benefits) are likely restricted.

C. After Market Ends:

  • Close down your stall — complete your last transaction
  • Complete End of Market Vendor Invoice Form ↗ (Note: Vendor invoices are only generated when subsidy-based benefits are used at the market. If your market does not use benefits, there is no end-of-market invoice form. Verify with your manager.)
  • Complete Reconciliation form/process (verify with your market manager if this applies to your market)
  • Update your item inventory
  • Complete the Upcoming Market Form again (for recurring markets)
  • Stay fresh on key program/market info and policies; refresh on use of transaction form, receipts, etc.
  • Check your vendor stats — see the Data Visualization module in the Additional Resources section
